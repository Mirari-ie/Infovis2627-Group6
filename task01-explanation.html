Task 1 explanation

In this task, we will be using a dataset containing information about accidents in Barcelona between 2019 and 2025.

Firstly, we classified the variables into three main groups:

  • Temporal data: the date and time of the accident.
  • Geographical data: the district, neighbourhood and street where the accident happened.
  • Accident-related data: the number of vehicles involved, the number of people injured or killed, the pedestrian-related cause, etc.

This made it easier for us to identify some overlapping variables, such as Descripcio_dia_setmana and Dia_setmana, Mes_any and Nom_mes, or Hora_dia and Descripcio_torn. It also helped us decide which relationships we wanted to explore.

After determining the data type and subtype of each variable, we created some charts to investigate whether there is a specific time of day or day of the week when most accidents occur, and whether the district makes a difference in the number of accidents.

To answer the first question, “Is there a specific time of day when most accidents happen, and does this differ between weekdays and weekends?”, we created a split violin showing the distribution of the number of accidents per hour, with weekdays and weekends represented using different colours. We observed that on weekdays, the number of accidents is higher during rush hours. However, at weekends, there is a decrease in the number of accidents in the morning and an increase in accidents late at night, compared to weekdays.

To answer the second question, “Is there a district where the majority of accidents happen?”, we created a heat map representing the districts and the number of accidents that occurred in each of them. By displaying the data on a map of Barcelona, we can appreciate the different sizes of the districts and, therefore, better understand the distribution of accidents across the city. Eixample stands out by far because, despite not being the largest district in terms of surface area, it has the highest number of recorded accidents (>14,000). It is followed by Sarrià, Sant Martí and Sants-Montjuïc (between 6,000 and 8,000 accidents). At the other end, Ciutat Vella, Gràcia and Nou Barris have the lowest number of accidents (<4,000).

Conclusion

Overall, our analysis shows that the occurrence of accidents in Barcelona seems to be related to both time and location. We observed that the number of accidents varies depending on the day of the week and the time of day, with different patterns between weekdays and weekends. We also found significant differences between districts. However, the number of accidents in each district may also be influenced by many factors, such as population density, traffic volume, number of streets and vehicles in the area, etc. Therefore, these results help us identify some patterns in Barcelona's accidents, but there are still many factors that we would need to take into account in order to reach a more complete conclusion.
