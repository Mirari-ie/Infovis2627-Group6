Task 1 visualisation

In this assignment, we did an exploratory data analysis on the use of social media, and on how it affects the dopamine levels and the productivity of the population. For that, we chose a dataset that contained all the data needed, and erased the columns that were not relevant for our purpose, so that the data would be more manageable.

To start with the analysis, we classified the data we kept, and saw that we could divide it in this main groups:

  • Demographical data: information about the age, age group, gender, education level or employment status.
  • Data concerning the social media use: the number of hours spent on average on social media, primary platforms, how much time people spend scrolling at night...
  • Data about the dopamine boost and the dependency related behaviour: the dopamine rush feel score, the fomo score, the validation seeking score...
  • Data about productivity: the attention span, how many minutes the deep work of a person lasts...
  • Other potentially relevant variables: the average hours of sleep or the sleep quality.

On the other hand, we made a second classification of the data, this time from the point of view of data analysis:

  • Categorical data: there is categorical ordinal data (the age group and late night scrolling time), as well as categorical nominal data (the gender, education level, employment status and primary platforms).
  • Numerical data: the age, the average daily hours in social media, the dopamine rush feel score, the fomo score, the validation seeking score, the attention span and the deep work duration. In this case, all the numerical data that we have is discrete.
    •

    Taking that into account, we made some graphics relating different variables, so that we could see if our phones and social media have affected our daily lives at all, and if so, which aspects are the most influenced ones.

    In general, we saw that there has been quite a big impact, and that it has been mainly negative, at least regarding the variables that we analysed. The most remarkable ones are how strongly the social media use decreases the attention span and that the more time people spend scrolling at night, the worse is their sleep quality (the correlations can be seen in the graphics).

    Finally, to summarize our findings, we made a heat map relating the average social media hours, dopamine levels, fomo score, validation score, attention span, deep work and sleep quality (it can be seen in the image too). The heat map showed that the first four variables are highly correlated with each other, and that the negative correlation with the other three variables is equally strong. So, overall, this shows that phones and social media have a negative impact on variables such as productivity and sleep quality, which could be analysed further in a more extensive study.

    Lastly, we wanted to mention that the dataset has been created with artificial intelligence analysed and that all the data is invented.
